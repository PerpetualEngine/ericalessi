The Contact Improvisation Canon

A directory I keep growing: the texts and films that shape my practice and my teaching, on seven shelves. Each entry says what the work argues and when to reach for it. Where a work has a free, legal home, the entry links to it; sources are being added shelf by shelf. Where a work is for sale, buy it.

The undertaking is deliberately big: to gather every interesting piece of work on Contact Improvisation and share it here, for the benefit of the global CI community. If something essential is missing, write to me.

One clarification on the paid entries: I earn nothing from anything listed here. No affiliate links, no commissions, and no association with any of the authors cited. I am sharing what I find interesting, that is all.

Shelf 1 · 6 entries Paxton in his own words The founder's own words, 1972–2013. Quote from these, not from commentaries.

The Grand Union · Steve Paxton, The Drama Review T-55, September 1972

Paxton's account, written in CI's birth season, of how Grand Union became leaderless: improvisation as the only form all could join as equals. Contains the circle-falling trust exercise with its training list (eyes' judgment, skin's sensitivity, timing, trust), a first-person description of bodies colliding and blending into one falling mass, and the statement that these dancers orient by weight and touch rather than the eye. CI's blueprint in print before the name existed.

If this library has one cornerstone, this is it.

Contact Improvisation / Solo Dancing / Grand Union · the three founding texts, 1972–1977 (French ed. "D'un pied sur l'autre," trans. Bigé, Recherches en danse 2017)

The 1975 piece is the founding technical statement: easiest pathways within mutually dependent masses, touch and balance as the base senses, the training list starting with attitude. "Solo Dancing" holds the canonical aphorisms: soloing doesn't exist, dancer plus floor is already a duet, two dancers make a quartet, weight is only yours to give.

Source · English originals in the CQ Sourcebook, $21 at Contact Editions

Drafting Interior Techniques · Paxton, Contact Quarterly 18.1, 1993

The founding document of interior technique: how he used verbal images to focus dancers inside sensation, the small dance, the gaps where consciousness blacks out mid-roll, the "working model" mover he taught toward. Imagining a step already displaces weight. Ends with a dedication to John Cage.

Read it before anything modern about attention.

Thoughts on Contact Improvisation · Paxton interviewed 1981, annotated by his 2013 self (ed. Mazzaglia, Danza e Ricerca 2013)

The deepest well of citable teaching language: gravity as the perceptual "field" with the moving point of contact as "figure"; feeling the floor through your partner's body as the principle CI rests on; the small dance introduced in 1972 so gravity could be observed working on the upright body. Plus his regret that standing has dropped out of practice, his replacement of "freedom" with "response-ability," and a sober late retraction of early utopianism.

The deepest well of teaching language on any shelf here.

Steve Paxton: Drafting Interior Techniques · ed. Emma Bigé, Culturgest, Lisbon 2019

The book. The retrospective's catalogue: Bigé's "fugitive study" introduction framing Paxton's decades as patient investigations; Cvejić's "A Study in Anarchy" arguing Paxton transferred authority from persons to gravity itself; and the Godard/Bigé "Moving-moved" conversation. Sardo's foreword lands on the idea that Paxton taught what Newton couldn't: what it is to be the apple. Free and legal in full via the editor's own deposit: Zenodo, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.10695529.

Source · free in the full 2019 book, the editor's own Zenodo deposit

Contact Improvisation and Politics · Nita Little with Peter Pleyer, 2013/2020, CQ Newsletter

Founder testimony: CI replaced modern dance's up/down hierarchy of weight with spirals where every direction has value; asking questions moment-by-moment is itself the politics; peripheral intelligence outruns centralized intelligence. Includes her eyewitness chronology: Magnesium seemed "for men only" until Paxton showed her the material; the Weber Gallery rehearsals were heavily videotaped and studied while still in body memory.

Shelf 2 · 8 entries The gravity school Where support comes from. The Floor as Sky shelf.

Moving-moved · Hubert Godard & Emma Bigé, in the 2019 Culturgest book

The single most important text on this shelf. The small dance as the discovery that we stream movements that aren't ours; tonic function rooted in the infant's tonic dialogue; CI as a "parietal dance" in which grounds communicate through bodies — my body makes a parcel of Earth perceivable to you; gravity as the repressed relation with no god in any pantheon. Mutual freedom with mutual dependence.

Read first. Slowly. Twice.

The Missing Gesture · Godard interview, 1994 (Writings on Dance 15, 1996)

The clinical side: recovered patients missing specific gestures no test detects; "founding gestures" (pushing away, pointing, going towards) each learned in an affective situation — the capacity to say no lives in the muscles that push away; gesture born from gravitational organization (raising an arm begins in the calf).

Phenomenological Space · Godard interviewed by McHose, Contact Quarterly 2006

Tonic function as half push from the floor, half suspension toward the sky — the ground-and-sky posture formula. The hamstring lengthens when you orient to ground and sky, not when you stretch it; CI named as the direct way to renegotiate the touch taboo.

Tonic Space · Emma Bigé, CQ Unbound 2017

The aesthetics of weight: upright posture as lifelong task (Straus), the small dance opening perception beneath volition, CI training "nomadic supports" — weight given spherically to any point. Where music gives tonal space, CI substitutes tonic space: coordinates defined by weight, dancers bathed in the gravitational field instead of sound.

Source · Emma Bigé's own site (texts + deposits)

Living Weight · Emma Bigé, "Le poids vivant," 2018FR

The scholarly full version: postural tonus, parent-infant tonic dialogue, each partner a floor, the Earth rediscovered as a power of attraction one dialogues with, every step a modulated arc of falling. Quotes Paxton's mirror-floor workshop score — the inverted body that supports you — which is Floor as Sky stated by the founder.

Touch and the Fall · Emma Bigé, "L'haptique et la chute," 2015FR

Against the "shared equilibrium" cliché: equilibrium is death; the partner's job is to help you lose your verticals. Up and down become relative to the partner's body — his back a floor, a wall, a ramp. Welcoming a partner and grounding yourself are one act.

The closest published cousin to what I teach.

Source · Emma Bigé's own site (texts + deposits)

Horizontal Gravity · Michael O'Connor, 2015

A speculative attempt to define the pull between living bodies as a second gravity, ending in Hennessy's "post/contact" (failure more than flow).

Leaning Bodies That Imagine · Ixiar Rozas, "Cuerpos inclinados que imaginan," 2020ES

Pandemic-era essay weaving Paxton's whole trajectory with Cavarero's "inclination": leaning toward gravity as critique of vertical, masculine rectitude.

Shelf 3 · 9 entries Attention & perception What attention actually is in this dance, and how it gets trained: vision, touch, and the research behind reading a partner faster than thought.

Before Your Eyes · Lisa Nelson, Contact Quarterly 29.1, 2004

The other founding lineage: vision as movement practice. Four years behind a camera taught her the eyes compose the body; she learned to catch the split-second of organization before an action appears; her group practice has watchers replay a dance with their bodies — a collective perception, a dance of opinions. She credits Paxton's 1972 survival question as her seed.

The journal's two-perspective format has an ancestor here.

Source · free full text at the Sarma archive

Restructuring the Self-Sensing · Nita Little, Journal of Dance & Somatic Practices 2014

Attention-to-attention as the training; trained attention slices a fall into many decisions where beginners get one chunk.

Source · ResearchGate (author-posted)

Bodyminds in Movement · Yotam Shibolet, 2018

CI as living lab for embodied cognition (Noë's enactive perception, Massumi's proprioceptive navigation); maps the physics-vs-chemistry split in CI's self-understanding; defends practitioner writing against neutral-spectator scholarship.

Expanding Empathic and Perceptive Awareness · Pini & Deans, 2021

Attunement (Stern's infant research) as what CI trains; the dual gaze; and mismatch-and-repair — imperfect attunement as the productive mechanism.

touch + talk · Malaika Sarco-Thomas, JDSP 2014

Lepkoff's CI-as-question made into a performable score: dancers answer a projected question by moving, touching and talking at once. The question menu doubles as journal prompts.

Momentum, gravity and "sensational facts" · Sarco-Thomas, 2015

The most compact teachable small-dance script in the set, plus Chung's fingertip "follow the one you lead" exercise with the space between fingers as third agent, and Little's attention "pop."

Real-time Authorship · Chrysa Parkinson & Emma Bigé, Contact Quarterly 2018

The dancer authors their experience without owning the work; the Stark Smith line that Paxton transmitted CI as a frame with the middle left empty.

On Kinesic Analysis · Daniel Stern, The Drama Review 1973

The infant researcher Paxton cited and visited: micro-synchrony, the mother's exaggerations as the first performance, film conquering time.

Dancer–Dance–Spirituality · Ramaswamy & Deslauriers, 2014

CI's unstated spiritual register given careful language: jams as hybrid of focused-attention and open-presence meditation; joining felt as movement from a third place.

Shelf 4 · 9 entries Consent, gender, hierarchy The hard conversations, in writing: touch and consent, power inside a form that claims to have none, and who the room actually welcomes.

Nuances of Touch: Embodying and Communicating Nonverbal Consent in CI · Brynn Williams, MA thesis, UBC

The only full-length study of nonverbal consent in CI in this library (121 pages; full annotation pending).

Gender Discourses in Contact Improvisation · María Paz Brozas-Polo, "Discursos de género," 2020ES

The definitive archaeology of the gender debate inside Contact Quarterly across 45 years, with a dated table of every relevant article.

To Lead or Be Led · Brozas-Polo, "Llevar o ser llevada," 2022ES

Tango's explicit roles vs CI's supposed equality: the jam's openness can amplify elite power and leave room for abuse; the Magnesium all-male origin story.

Feeling and Moving Together · Emma Bigé, "Sentir et se mouvoir ensemble," 2015FR

The most clear-eyed insider text on CI's shadows: in-group language that discourages dissent, dress-code academicism, beginner humiliation, male-dominated teaching in female-majority communities — resolved through the small dance's community of sensing.

Required reading before writing a jam's code of conduct.

Uneasy Breathing · Chrys Papaioannou, 2024/25

The strongest recent challenge to "just relax into gravity": Holland and Houston-Jones's 1983 Wrong Contact Manifesto; Paxton's uninhibited "imaginary person" read as an unmarked white subject; Nodine's observation that flow is a form of privilege. Tension reframed as socially produced.

[Dis]Connected · Erin Stahmer, Barnard thesis 2011

A sympathetic audit of the universal-language claim: communication is real but conditional; hierarchies re-emerge; touch can turn coercive; and a documented decline of the small dance in teaching.

Dancing Anarchy · Emma Bigé, "Danser l'Anarchie," 2020FR

Post-modern dance wasn't democratic, it was anarchist: Judson's anti-institutionalism, Grand Union's temporary autonomous zone, CI's mutualism via Kropotkin — whom Paxton cites directly. Roles must form and never congeal.

A Physical Quest for Natural Rights · Bojana Cvejić, in the 2019 Culturgest book

Paxton didn't remove authority from CI — he transferred it to gravity. Includes his pedagogical redirect (toward curiosity about momentum instead of prohibitions) and the letter arguing CI addresses sexism and racism by providing a proposition in which they are irrelevant.

Source · free in the full 2019 book on Zenodo

How the First Rule Brought #MeToo to Contact Improvisation · Michelle Beaulieux, Contact Quarterly 44.1

The article that brought the reckoning into CI's own journal of record: how the form's founding "take care of yourself" rule can offload responsibility onto the least powerful person in the room, and what communities changed after #MeToo. Pairs with her response essay "Starting by Believing Maria."

The most important consent text of the last decade.

Source · free PDF at Contact Quarterly

Shelf 5 · 8 entries Jams, pedagogy, accessibility The Brozas shelf: María Paz Brozas-Polo (Universidad de León), historian of CI in Spain, plus the Buenos Aires school.

The Aesthetic and Pedagogical Nature of Jams · Brozas-Polo, "Naturaleza estética y pedagógica de las jams," 2014ES

The sharpest anatomy of the jam as pedagogical device: a five-type map of what organizers think jams are for, and the documented drift toward beginner-social jams that experienced dancers abandon.

Pedagogy of the Sensitive Body · Brozas-Polo, "Pedagogía del cuerpo sensible," 2017ES

A decade of CQ "Essentials" teaching scores (Chung, Nelson, Little, Keogh, Lepkoff…) read as a pedagogy of touch: deep, extended, multiple, porous, spatial, internal touch; her closing challenge that CI never worked out what vision does.

Accessibility in Contact Improvisation · Brozas-Polo, "La accesibilidad en la danza CI," 2013ES

Accessibility as founding principle, with the full lineage (DanceAbility, blind-dancer work, Exposed to Gravity) dated and cited — plus the honest caveat that touch remains a social barrier.

The Beginnings of CI in Spain · Brozas & García, "Los comienzos de la danza CI en España," 2014ES

How CI actually roots in a new country: multiple unaware seeds, migration, one journal as connective tissue.

CI and the Health Sciences · Sarco-Thomas & Zammit

The Malta pilot: 8 weeks of CI for nursing students produced a measurable shift from self-only awareness to awareness of others' bodies.

Critical Potential · Destabilizing the Discipline · CI as Communication · the Buenos Aires school: Menacho 2008 · Tampini & Farina ~2011 · Singer 2021ES

The Buenos Aires school, three philosophical readings: Marcuse (CI as lived non-manipulative relation), Foucault (CI destabilizing the discipline that produces docile bodies), Deleuze/Spinoza (the "third entity" as composition of relations that increases each body's power).

Gestures of Contact Improvisation · Emma Bigé, "Gestes du CI," Musée de la danse 2019FR

A museum of dancing organized around seven gestures: not-doing, weighing/carrying, falling, playing, saying, looking, touching.

The Underscore · Nancy Stark Smith's long-form jam score

Stark Smith's structure for a whole jam as one composed arc, developed from 1990 on and practiced worldwide every June as the Global Underscore. Her own page explains the practice; the famous one-page glyph sheet maps its phases in her hand-drawn symbols.

The map for designing a long session.

Source · her official Underscore page · the glyph sheet (PDF)

Shelf 6 · 9 entries History & scenes Where the form came from, and the unexpected rooms it reached: galleries, mourning, forests, and fifty years of scenes.

The Body as an Everyday Material · Archias, 2010

Pre-CI Paxton: why walking, why boredom, the Cunningham break.

New forms of queer mourning · Ramírez Tur & López del Rincón, 2023

The Touching Community: CI as mourning technology for dancers lost to AIDS — the most striking case of CI beyond the studio.

Companion · Hennessy & Houston-Jones in conversation on dancing through the AIDS years (SFMOMA Open Space)

Sensing with Trees · Rufo, 2022

CI-derived solo practice with trees; directly usable on the island.

On a journey, backwards · Georgelou, 2009

Late Paxton; his fluid-mass gravity mental state.

Soft Class · Papaioannou, 2024

The small dance read through the undercommons; confirms the Oberlin dawn Soft Class dating.

Bodily Diversity · Brozas & Vicente-Pedraz, "La diversidad corporal," 2017ES

The century-long canon-breaking arc landing on CI.

The Experiment Called Contact Improvisation · FoundSF, the open San Francisco history archive

How CI rooted in the Bay Area, told through the city's own open history project: the scene, the spaces, the people who carried the form west.

Source · read at FoundSF

Researching Contact Improvisation · the Five Ways In documentary project, University of Kent

The research platform behind the anthropological CI documentary Five Ways In (Poltorak, Lynes, Brühlmann): video research, themes from community to political change, and a blog running to 2020.

Source · researchingcontactimprovisation.com
